The market you're in

The numbers, not the vibes

You're not imagining it. This is what you're up against.

  • 33%of US game-industry workers laid off in the last two years, per GDC's 2026 State of the Game Industry survey
  • 48%of those laid off are still looking for work — 36% of them for over a year, per the same survey
  • 1 in 2workers say their employer cut staff in the past 12 months, per GDC 2026

Epic Games cut about a thousand roles this year. Bungie cut roughly four hundred. Ubisoft cut roughly six hundred and eighty across its studios. Every one of those people is applying into the same pool you are — which is why postings that used to stay open for weeks now often close in days: there's no shortage of candidates left for a studio to keep reading.

From the founder

I went through a round like these myself. The studio I worked at for nine years shrank from forty people to fifteen, and I left soon after. I built the first version of this for my own search, months before I offered it to anyone else — it's the tool I needed, not a stranger's idea of one.

Asked by people in your position

The obvious objections, answered.

Isn't this just trading on a bad situation?

No — it was built inside one. The founder went through a layoff himself and ran it on his own search first, months before it was anyone else's.

Is this just another job board?

No — it doesn't compete on how many listings it has. It judges each one against criteria you actually wrote, the way a recruiter working for you would, if you had one who replied.

Does it only cover games?

Yes, deliberately. It's tuned to this industry's studios, engines, and hiring patterns specifically — not a generic filter with a genre checkbox.

Which disciplines?

Any game-industry role: engineering, art, design, production, audio, QA. It judges against your brief, not a fixed list of job titles.

Where does it look?

Job boards, studio careers pages and the game-industry sources it can reach. Some networks keep their listings behind a login, and those it can't read. Each alert says where the posting was found.

How fast will I hear about a posting?

As soon as it has found and judged it — it checks continuously, not once a week. You won't get a promised number of days; you will get a hunter that never waits for the weekend.

What if it gets something wrong?

It shows its work — the score and a verdict on every criterion, with anything it couldn't verify marked exactly that, never dressed up as certainty. Disagree with one tap; if it keeps missing the same way, that's a line to add to your brief.

Does it get me hired?

No. It finds and judges roles; applying and interviewing stay yours. Anyone promising more than that in this market is selling you something.

Am I the only one seeing these leads?

No. Anyone with a similar brief sees overlapping leads — exclusivity isn't a thing anyone can honestly sell. The edge is arriving early, judged against your own brief.

What happens to my CV?

It's read to build your brief and kept to judge postings against you. It's never sold, traded or handed to a third party — the one exception is the AI that runs the hunt, which reads it for that purpose only. No studio ever sees it.

What will it cost?

About what a coffee costs, monthly, cancel any time. It runs on inference cost, not a sales team — priced for people with less room to spend right now. A donation option exists too, for anyone who'd rather pay once this is over.

Which apps does it message?

Telegram at launch. Discord support is planned. If neither works for you, email is the fallback.
